Welcome to the quarterly engineering review. Today I will cover five areas: hiring, pricing, the database migration, our incident record, and what we are planning for next quarter.
Let us start with hiring. We closed the quarter with fourteen engineers on staff. We plan to add six more before the end of the year, mostly on the platform team. Recruiting has been slow, because the market for infrastructure people is extremely competitive right now. We lost two candidates to larger companies purely on compensation. Our average time to hire is now sixty two days, which is up from forty days last year. To fix this we are raising the base band for senior engineers and we are opening a second office so we are not competing only in one city.
Now let us talk about pricing, and this is the big one. We moved away from per seat licensing to a usage based model in March. Customers now pay for the number of documents they process, rather than for the number of people on the account. Revenue went up nineteen percent in the first two months, and churn dropped by almost half. The reason churn dropped is that small teams are no longer punished for adding a colleague. Under the old model, every new person was another line on the invoice, so accounts would quietly share logins instead of growing. Under usage based pricing, growth in headcount costs the customer nothing, and we capture value only when they actually get value.
Third, the database migration. We finished moving off the single Postgres instance onto a sharded cluster in June. Read latency at the ninety ninth percentile fell from four hundred milliseconds down to about sixty milliseconds. Write throughput roughly tripled. The migration took eleven weeks, which was three weeks longer than we estimated, and almost all of the overrun went into backfilling old rows and verifying that the shard keys were balanced.
Fourth, incidents. We had four incidents this quarter. One of them happened during the database cutover. It lasted eleven minutes and affected roughly two percent of requests. The other three were much smaller: a bad deployment that we rolled back in six minutes, an expired certificate on an internal service, and a third party payment provider outage that was entirely outside our control. Our overall availability for the quarter was ninety nine point nine six percent, which is above our target.
Finally, next quarter. The main project is multi region failover. Right now everything runs in a single region, and a regional outage would take us down completely. We are also going to invest in the search experience, because it is the single most common complaint in our support tickets. And we will finish the security audit that we started in May, which should close out before the end of November.
That is everything. Thank you all for a very strong quarter, and I am happy to take questions.
